About

Virtual design, real machines.

I build the virtual version of a factory before the steel is ordered — and then I go on site and commission the real one. My work sits where simulation, control engineering and software meet: discrete-event models that expose a bottleneck, kinematic machine models wired to a live PLC over OPC UA, and desktop applications that turn customer data into machine instructions.

At Wiksfors Technology I owned automation projects end to end — Visual Components and Tecnomatix Plant Simulation studies, Beckhoff TwinCAT 3 logic, ABB and KUKA robot cells, C#/WPF applications — for customers in Sweden and the United States. Today I'm embedded at Volvo Cars Torslanda as an automation consultant, keeping a live battery assembly line running: diagnosing faults from field sensors through PLC logic and robot programs up to HMI sequencing, and writing the SOPs that capture what we learn.

What I care about is the handover moment — when the model matches the machine, the FAT passes and the customer's operators can run it without me in the room.

Sep — Nov 2023🇸🇪 Säffle

SkruvPortal — Multi-Bridge Timber Machine

Verkstadsbolaget AB · Wiksfors Technology

End-to-end ownership of virtual design, PLC logic validation, OPC UA data handling and on-site commissioning of a multi-gantry CNC machine that nails and routes prefabricated timber wall elements.

Visual ComponentsTwinCAT 3OPC UA.NET / WPFFAT
Open the case →▶ Video

The machine processes wall data exported from the customer's MES as .BTL files (Building Transfer Language, the timber industry's CAD/CAM standard), validates and translates them into PLC operations, and runs nailing, routing and stud-straightening across two work tables with up to two layers per wall.

Virtual machine design

Built a full kinematic and process model of the Multi Bridge in Visual Components — the X-axis portal, four nailing tools on independent Y-axes, the routing spindle with Rx rotation, the stud straightener and Festo pneumatic actuation — including both work tables and the two-layer workflow.

PLC logic & virtual commissioning

Programmed and tested control logic in Beckhoff TwinCAT 3 (with parts of the cell on a Mitsubishi GX Works PLC), wired through OPC UA to the simulation. Function blocks implemented and verified:

  • Continuous nailing in X (top/bottom plate) and Y (stud nailing)
  • Rectangular and circular routing plus spiral "rout-to-dust" variants
  • Linear horizontal / vertical routing executed by portal X-motion
  • Stud-straightener synchronisation with the portal
  • Pause function and emergency-stop / resume control logic
  • Tool-zone control preventing Y-axis tool collisions

Custom data-handling application

Co-developed and tested the Wiksfors Data Handler (PortalFormApp — .NET Framework 4.8, WPF, ClickOnce-deployed, version line 1.0.0.74 → 1.0.0.106). The app watches a PostOffice/Inbox folder, validates each BTL against PortalMachineConfig.xml and routes files to Queue (accepted) or NotMatchingMachine (rejected) with full logging. It exposes ~20 defined exception types — "Wall does not fit the machine", "Layer1: all vertical routing lines are not straight", "Material not found in material config" — and surfaces them on the HMI, speaking to the PLC over OPC UA on a dedicated 192.168.10.0/24 subnet.

Test campaign & commissioning

Ran 70+ real BTL test walls through the simulation, logged bugs in a tracked sheet and closed them iteratively, and performed axis-scaling calibration runs in both travel directions. On site in Säffle I drove a ~40-item commissioning checklist covering stud-straightener calibration, gypsum nailing-pressure tuning, automatic Z-position adjustment per layer, automatic tool soft-limits derived from wall width, and spindle start sequence and "ready" interlocks — concluding with a successful FAT and physical hand-over.

Nov 2023 — Jan 2024🇺🇸 Idaho

Roof Truss Factory Simulation — AMD-1000

House of Design Robotics · Wiksfors Technology

A parametric, high-fidelity simulation of a robotic roof-truss assembly line — one underlying scene generating all six product configurations, used both as the customer's internal sizing tool and as a sales asset.

Visual ComponentsABB IRB 6700ABB IRB 1200Cycle time
Open the case →▶ Video

House of Design offers the AMD-1000 in six physical configurations (left/right material feed × single/dual gantry × single/dual station table), so the simulation had to support the whole product family from one model rather than six standalone scenes.

Parametric layout

Modelled the complete robotic factory layout and production flow in Visual Components, parameterised so that all six variants generate from a single scene. That gave the customer side-by-side comparable cycle-time and utilisation metrics across configurations instead of six unrelated visualisations.

Two-robot architecture

Specified and integrated the line's two robots: an ABB IRB 6700-155/3.05 (155 kg payload, 3.05 m reach) gantry-mounted for chord pick-and-place — typical residential roof-truss chords exceed 8 m, so the part demands both reach and payload — paired with an ABB IRB 1200-5/0.9 (5 kg, 0.9 m) for nail-plate placement, chosen for speed and accuracy over short distances.

Production flow modelling

Modelled conveyor sequencing, gantry travel along the staging table, input-rack feed timing and output-buffer dynamics, so cycle time, robot utilisation and queue lengths could be read directly from the simulation and used to size line throughput.

Virtual cell design

Built the full layout in Visual Components: 10 m × 5 m floor footprint, 8 m × 1.8 m robot work envelope, 1 m fence stand-off zone and an external 2 m × 2.1 m operator control room outside the perimeter. KUKA commissioning support
